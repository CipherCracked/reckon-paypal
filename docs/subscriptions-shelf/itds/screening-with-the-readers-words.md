# ITD 36 — Screening With the Reader's Own Words

| ITD 36 — *"Use the reader's existing vocabulary, applied to clauses of a listing's own text, to decide which catalogue results belong on a shelf of things that repeat — with no model call on the shelf."* |  |
| :---- | :---- |
| **THE PROBLEM** | IPD 41 decides the shelf admits a listing because its own words say it repeats, and quotes those words on the tile. Something has to do the admitting, and the catalogue's search cannot: `POST /v1/search` is a nearest-neighbour match, so *monthly subscription box* returned, in one page of thirty, four beauty boxes, three BarkBox plans, four soap subscriptions — and three wall calendars and a book. Search `subscription` alone is worse: books about subscriptions, extended service plans, tax forms. <br><br> The model is the product's real reader and costs ~40 s per listing on the endpoint the demo runs on, which is why the shop's cards have never been read (IPD 40). Reading thirty tiles is half an hour. <br><br> What exists already: `lib/commitment/published-term.ts` holds the vocabulary that decides whether a period is an agreement — `AGREEMENT`, `NOT_AN_AGREEMENT` checked first, `BILLING_OR_DELIVERY` for cadences — and the two parsers `parseStatedLength` and `parseCadence` in `lib/document/parse.ts`, which work on any text. Those checks were written for verified model spans, but nothing in them is specific to a model. |
| **OPTIONS CONSIDERED (Decision in bold)** | 1. **Export the reader's meaning checks as a text-level screen, apply them to clauses of each result's title and description, and keep the first clause that passes.** <br> 2. Write a shelf-specific keyword list — *subscription, monthly, club, box, delivered*. <br> 3. Read each result with the model in the background and cache the answers. <br> 4. Ask the catalogue a narrower query and trust its ordering. |
| **REASONING** | The deciding factor is **one vocabulary, or two**. The reader's checks already refuse the cases that matter: *2-Year Extended Service Coverage* fails because a warranty is not an agreement, and *2027 Cat in a Box Monthly Wall Calendar* fails because a calendar states a month and charges nothing. A shelf with its own keyword list would admit both — the calendars literally contain *monthly* — and the product page would then refuse what the shelf had just offered, which is the product contradicting itself in two screens. Option 1 makes the products' refusals shared, and lets the shelf say, truthfully, that it shows the words the page will read. <br><br> The second factor is **how much of a result to show**. A whole sentence is often a hundred characters of marketing, and a tile's quote has to be short enough to read without becoming a paragraph. The screen therefore splits each text into clauses — on sentence ends, commas, semicolons and dashes — and keeps the first clause that passes, title before description. *The Detox Box 6-Month Subscription* passes on the title with six words; *billed and shipped on a quarterly basis* passes on a clause of the description. <br><br> The third factor is **what happens when the screen fails**. A search that throws and a shelf that comes back empty are different answers to a shopper (IPD 26's distinction, the same one `resolveProductOutcome` draws), so the reader throws only when every query failed, and answers an empty list when the catalogue answered and nothing passed. <br><br> Option 2 is rejected because it is the second opinion above. Option 3 is rejected on measured cost and on the fact that a shelf must be the same shelf for two people in the same minute (IPD 41, Options 2 and 4). Option 4 is rejected because a query is a request, not a filter: *subscription* is thirty results and one box. |
| **TRADEOFFS** | The screen's precision and its recall are both bounded by the vocabulary. *Billed quarterly* is admitted; *renews each spring* is not, and neither is a listing whose copy never names a period. The shelf is therefore a sample, and IPD 42 puts the sentence that says so above the grid. <br><br> Clause selection is a reading of convenience: the first passing clause is not always the most informative one, and a title that passes hides a description that would have said more. It is also the only rule that can be explained in a sentence and tested without a model. <br><br> Two searches cost two catalogue calls per cold visit, 5 minutes of cache each. The shelf is as fresh as a category page and no fresher. |
| **NOTES** | The screen runs on `listingText`'s own text — title and description joined — so the words a tile quotes are exactly the words the product page's reading is keyed on. <br><br> Results are deduplicated by catalogue id, first query winning, and capped at one page's worth. No ordering is applied: the catalogue's order is kept, because any rank of ours would be a claim about which subscriptions matter. <br><br> **As built, 2026-11-12.** The screen lives in `published-term.ts` beside the vocabulary it uses (`repeatingWords`), so a future term rule cannot be added without the shelf seeing it. `readRepeatingShelf` in `lib/market/repeating.ts` owns the queries, the dedupe and the cap; `app/shop/subscriptions/page.tsx` is the screen, reusing the shop's own failure sentences from `lib/market/shop-words.ts`. <br><br> Measured against the live catalogue through the dev server: the shelf rendered **22 tiles**, every one a product that charges again — BarkBox plans, the Detox Box, four beauty boxes, four soap subscriptions, a candle club — with the three wall calendars, both Total Care protection plans, the books and the tax forms refused. Two defects the live read found, both fixed: quotes were cutting at a merchant's abbreviation (*"A quarterly subscription from Dr"*), so sentences are now joined across `Dr`, `Inc`, `St` and their kin; and quotes are **sentences**, abridged at a word boundary with an ellipsis rather than comma-fragments, because *"…delivering five curated"* read as broken text rather than as an excerpt. <br><br> The catalogue's search ranking is **not stable between identical calls**: two fetches a minute apart returned 22 and 21 tiles, and the two listings that vanished were absent from the raw results rather than refused by the screen. The shelf is a sample, and the sentence above the grid says what it is. |
