# IPD — Assistance Model

**Status:** Proposed (revised)
**Date:** 2026-11-05
**Answers:** Problem tree root question 3 — *What should assistance look like — explain, decide, act, or support?*

---

## Problem to solve

At the moment of consent the product decides two things: which quantities it puts in front of the shopper, and whether it confirms that the shopper understood them.

Dyscalculia removes the ability to judge an amount, not the right to decide. Show too little and the shopper is unaided at the moment that matters. Show too much, or decide on their behalf, and the product replaces the judgement it was meant to restore.

---

## Options

### **Option 1 — The full commitment, with an optional recognition check**

The product states three quantities: charged today, the cost across the term, and the cost of leaving. A breakdown sits beneath them. After the shopper has read the total, the product offers one optional check — whether the amount matches what they expected — and confirms or corrects.

### Option 2 — The full commitment, without confirmation

The same three quantities, presented and left alone. The shopper reads and decides.

### Option 3 — A mandatory comprehension gate

The shopper must correctly identify the amount before the payment can proceed. Nothing is charged until the check is passed.

### Option 4 — Commitment translation with advice

The three quantities arrive alongside a judgement about whether the commitment is worth making.

---

## Reasoning

Three quantities, because each answers a different question the disability blocks. What is charged today is the figure the page emphasises. What the commitment costs across its term is the derived quantity the shopper cannot hold. What it costs to leave is the quantity nobody computes and the one that most often changes whether a commitment is wise.

The check is optional because making it mandatory turns assistance into a test the shopper can fail, and a person barred from paying by their own disability has been excluded rather than helped. Left optional, it does something a display cannot: it confirms that the number landed, and it makes the product's effect measurable rather than asserted.

Advice stays out for the reason it always has. Affordability and value depend on circumstances the product cannot see, and a shopper told what to do learns to defer rather than to judge.

---

## Tradeoffs

Three quantities and a breakdown carry more visual weight than a single total, at the busiest moment in the flow. The optional check can be ignored, which weakens its value as a measure.

The exit cost depends on cancellation terms merchants sometimes do not publish. When the product cannot determine it, it must say so rather than estimate — which means the most useful quantity is occasionally the one missing.

---

## Notes

- The recognition check presents the amount and asks whether it matches expectation. It never asks the shopper to produce the figure: recognition survives dyscalculia, recall does not.
- The check records nothing beyond the session, consistent with IPD 4.
