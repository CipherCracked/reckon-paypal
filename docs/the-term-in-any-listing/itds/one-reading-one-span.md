# ITD 33 — One Reading, One Span, and a Term Only If the Words Say So

| ITD 33 — *"Read a listing once, ask the model for the sentence that states the term, and parse a schedule only out of a span that passes both the verification and a meaning check."* |  |
| :---- | :---- |
| **THE PROBLEM** | The reader's vocabulary has no word for a term length. `FIELDS` (`lib/document/fields.ts:7–24`) names eight things a document may say — a total, an amount per period, a count, a cadence, an extra, an exit fee, a free exit, a due date — and `period` (`:15`) is the cadence, not the length. Nothing in that list can hold *"6-Month Subscription"*. <br><br> The listing path needs the term *and* the exit figure, and today they would be two readings of the same text: `readPublishedExit` calls `requestQuotations` (`lib/commitment/published-exit.ts:166`), and a new term reader would call it again. The model is the slowest thing in the app — roughly 47 seconds measured on the current endpoint — so two calls per listing doubles the wait a shopper sees (IPD 39) and halves the number of listings a warm-up can reach. <br><br> The schema is deliberately word-only (`lib/ai/read-document.ts:7–11`): the model may copy spans and nothing else. That rule is what makes a term readable at all, and it means a term can only arrive as a sentence that the server then has to interpret. The interpretation is where the danger is. The document path already has scars for exactly this (`lib/document/read.ts:34–80`): a live span *"You are agreeing to 24 months of service"* was offered as the sentence stating a free exit, and the page printed `$0.00` for a contract with a $180.00 fee in the next line. A verified span is not a verified meaning. <br><br> And a listing's text is not a contract's. In one search of thirty results for *subscription*, the periods and cadences came from books, warranties, wall calendars and tax forms: *"Total Care Program 1-Year Warranty"*, *"Sony Protect Plus … 3 Year"*, *"2027 Cat in a Box Monthly Wall Calendar"*. Every one would parse. None is a schedule. |
| **OPTIONS CONSIDERED (Decision in bold)** | 1. **Add `termLength` to the shared field list, read a listing once for term, cadence and exit together, verify every span, and accept a term only when the span states a duration *and* carries evidence that the duration belongs to an agreement.** <br> 2. A second tool and a second prompt for listings, leaving the document reader untouched. <br> 3. Let the model return the count and the period as numbers, alongside the spans. <br> 4. Keep two readings — the existing exit reader and a new term reader — so each stays small. |
| **REASONING** | **One text, one reading.** The term and the exit are facts about the same paragraph, and the model charges the same 47 seconds for either. Asking once means the listing's whole reading lands together: the shape, the figures and the sentence under them, in one cached result. Option 4 doubles the wait the shopper is already paying and makes the warm-up able to reach half as many listings. <br><br> **The span stays the only answer.** Option 3 is refused because it is the one thing this pipeline exists to prevent: a number the model produced is a number nobody can check, and the product's claim is that every figure traces to a sentence. The parser already exists — `parseCount` and `parsePeriod` (`lib/document/parse.ts:50`, `:62`) turn a span into the two parts of a schedule, refusing two different numbers in one span, and they are the same readers the document path uses. <br><br> **A duration is not a term until it is about an agreement.** This is the new decision in this document, and it is a meaning check laid on top of verification, the same shape the exit path already uses: `saysLeavingIsFree` requires both a leaving word and a no-charge phrase (`lib/document/read.ts:61–63`), because a real sentence labelled wrongly must not become a figure. A term span must pass two tests: it states a duration (a number — digits or a spelled one — next to month(s)/year(s)), and it carries an agreement word or a billing verb (subscription, subscribe, membership, plan, renews, recurring, auto-ship, prepaid, charged, billed). A coverage word in the same span — warranty, coverage, protection, guarantee — or a publication word — issue, volume, calendar, magazine — disqualifies it. <br><br> **A cadence gets the same treatment from the other side.** A cadence word alone (*"Monthly Wall Calendar"*) would put a calendar in the *repeats, no end* state (IPD 40). The cadence span must contain a billing or delivery verb — bill, charge, pay, ship, deliver, send, renew, arrive — or the listing's term span must say subscription or plan. *"Billed and shipped on a quarterly basis"* passes; *"2027 Cat in a Box Monthly Wall Calendar"* does not, and becomes what it is: a one-time purchase. <br><br> Option 2 is refused because two prompts and two field lists drift: the exit reader and the term reader would disagree about what a span is, and the shared enum is the single vocabulary that keeps a reading checkable. The listing prompt can differ in its framing — a listing is a title and a description, not a pasted contract — while the schema and the check stay one. |
| **TRADEOFFS** | A ninth field in the shared enum means every document reading can now name a term. The document path ignores it until it is adopted deliberately (`lib/document/read.ts:123–161` has no case for it), so `/read` is unchanged by this work — but the field's description is now part of the prompt a pasted contract sees, and the model will occasionally offer a term span there that nothing uses. <br><br> The guard words are English and hand-picked, and they will be wrong on listings nobody anticipated. The failure is designed to fall on the safe side: a span that fails the guard is not a term, so the listing reads as one-time rather than as a schedule with the wrong length. <br><br> One reading shares one failure. If the model is unavailable, the term and the exit are both absent for that listing, where two reads could have delivered one of them. The page's fourth state (IPD 40) is written for that case, and it is true of both figures at once. <br><br> Spelled numbers need a closed list. The catalogue really does write *"Six Month"*, so the term parser needs word-numbers where `parseCount` only reads digits; the list stays small (one through twenty-four), and a number outside it is not a term. |
| **NOTES** | The span travels with the term, as the exit's does (ITD 19): the accepted quotation is what the screen prints under the figure, through `QuotedText`, exactly as the curated map's quote does today — so a wrong reading is visible as the merchant's own sentence sitting under a wrong total, which is the correction path as much as the evidence path. <br><br> The text sent is the title and the description joined, matching what `termIsStated` already checks (`lib/market/subscriptions.ts:51–56`). A title-only term (*"BARK 12 Month Subscription"*) is as readable as a body one. <br><br> `termLength` is the field's name, and it holds a span — no field may hold a number the model computed. The unit of the term is the unit of the schedule only under IPD 38's rule, and the parser does not decide that — it reports what the span said. <br><br> What the reader now answers for one listing: `termLength` (a duration), `period` (a cadence), `exitFee` / `freeToLeave` (unchanged). Everything else in `FIELDS` belongs to the document path. |
