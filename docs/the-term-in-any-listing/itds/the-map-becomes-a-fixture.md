# ITD 34 — The Map Becomes a Fixture, Not the Gate

| ITD 34 — *"Resolve every retail listing through the reading, keep the four curated ids as a checked fixture for the screens that cannot wait, and give the resolution a named state instead of a shape it guessed."* |  |
| :---- | :---- |
| **THE PROBLEM** | One lookup decides what a retail listing is. `curatedTerm(listed.id)` (`lib/catalog/resolve.ts:111`) answers from a four-entry record (`lib/market/subscriptions.ts:27–37`); a hit builds a recurring commitment, a miss builds `marketTerms(offer)` (`:134`) — one payment, exit `none`, and the screen prints *"Paid once, so there is nothing to cancel"*. The product page repeats the decision (`app/product/[slug]/page.tsx:68–73`), and `tests/catalog-resolve.test.ts:135` pins it in words: *"keeps a listing the map does not curate as a one-time purchase."* <br><br> After IPDs 37–40 the decision comes from a reading, and the reading is slow and can fail (47 s measured, IPD 39). `resolveProduct` is called by seven places with different tolerances: the front door (`app/page.tsx`), the shop's link redirect and the product page, and four routes that move money or record it — the commitment route, orders, subscriptions, the calendar, and the receipt (`app/receipt/[orderId]/page.tsx:72`). A page may wait most of a minute for the reading because the reading is its subject. A capture must never wait, and a receipt must never turn a record into an absence. <br><br> `ProductLookup` already separates three failures of the catalogue read — `found`, `absent`, `unreadable` (`lib/catalog/resolve.ts:191–193`) — but it has no vocabulary for *the listing was read and it does not state a term*, *the term it states cannot be scheduled*, or *the reading has not happened yet*. Without those, a caller's only options are to guess a shape or to throw. |
| **OPTIONS CONSIDERED (Decision in bold)** | 1. **Read the listing inside resolution, return a named term state beside a nullable schedule, and keep the curated map for the cases where no reading exists — consulted only when the reading is absent and only while its span is still in the live record.** <br> 2. Keep the map for its four ids, and read only when the map misses. <br> 3. Delete the map, since the reading supersedes it. <br> 4. Leave resolution alone and read only in the product page. |
| **REASONING** | **One source of truth per fact.** Option 2 leaves two mechanisms answering one question, with no rule for disagreement: a curated listing whose description has since been edited to *"billed quarterly"* would be six monthly payments by the map and unschedulable by the reading. The rule that resolves it has to be written anyway, and the only defensible version is *the reading wins, because the reading is the text in front of us* — at which point the map is not a gate, it is a fallback, which is Option 1. <br><br> **The fallback has to exist, because two screens must answer before any reading does.** The front door opens on a fixture listing, and a cold server would otherwise greet the first visitor with a wait or an absence. The map's entries are not stale — all four ids answered `200` on 2026-11-12 and all four still contain the sentence the term was read from — and `termIsStated` (`lib/market/subscriptions.ts:51–56`) already re-checks the span against the live record on every resolution. A fixture that is verified against the thing it describes cannot quietly become a lie; it can only stop being usable, which is exactly when the reading takes over. Option 3 would trade that for a front door that is cold at the worst possible moment. <br><br> **The caller decides how long it waits, and the type makes it decide.** Every existing caller assumes `terms` is present; a nullable schedule moves that assumption into a compile error at each one, which is where the policy belongs. The product page waits (it is the page's subject). The consent, order, subscription and calendar routes take what is remembered and refuse with a sentence when there is nothing — a request that is about to move money cannot spend a minute on a model. The receipt prefers the PayPal plan's own terms (`app/receipt/[orderId]/page.tsx:170`), so a cold cache cannot erase a schedule that PayPal recorded at consent. <br><br> **Option 4 is refused on the money path.** The consent route resolves the slug to learn what it is charging; if the product page read the term and resolution did not, the plan would be created from a term the route never verified. A reading that does not travel with the terms is a reading the payment path cannot rely on. |
| **TRADEOFFS** | `resolveProduct` stops being a fast, pure catalogue read. Every caller now carries a latency policy, and the wrong policy is invisible until a request either hangs or says *could not read* when it could have waited. The default must be the cheap one, and the product page must opt in explicitly. <br><br> A nullable `terms` is a wide change: seven call sites, their tests, and any screen that reaches through to `product.terms`. The compiler finds them; a missing case becomes a runtime absence on a money screen. <br><br> The map's guarantee dilutes. It is no longer *these four are subscriptions*; it is *these four are known-good readings, used when nothing better is available*. The regression suite keeps its value, the coverage claim does not. <br><br> A merchant can change a listing's shape by editing a sentence, and the change lands on the next reading rather than on a deploy. That is the point of the decision and it is also a way for the demo to break an hour before the video. |
| **NOTES** | **The tests this rewrites, named in `todo.md` §19:** the four curated listings still resolve to their schedules (from the live record, as today); two catalogue-found subscriptions that were never curated are read and scheduled; and one listing whose term sentence has been rewritten resolves as *no term stated* rather than from the old quote. `tests/catalog-resolve.test.ts:135` changes from *"keeps a listing the map does not curate as a one-time purchase"* to the new behaviour — rewritten, not loosened — and the suite can drive every case by mocking `@/lib/ai/read-document`, as `tests/published-exit.test.ts:16` already does. <br><br> `marketTerms` survives, narrowed: it may only be returned when the reading found no term and no cadence. Its note changes with IPD 40 — *"Paid once, so there is nothing to cancel"* becomes a finding rather than a default. <br><br> `warm.ts` is unchanged in shape and richer in effect: the four fixture listings and the file texts are read at start-up, and each reading now returns the term as well as the exit. <br><br> The product page's rewritten-listing branch (`app/product/[slug]/page.tsx:73–86`) is superseded: *no term stated* is now one of the five states, not an error page, and it is reached by the same path whether the listing was curated or not. |
