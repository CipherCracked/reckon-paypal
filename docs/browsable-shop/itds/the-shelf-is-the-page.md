# ITD 31 — The Shelf Is the Page

| ITD 31 — *"Use the frame's full width, a grid of category links and a vertical product tile to make the shop browsable at a glance on a phone and a desk."* |  |
| :---- | :---- |
| **THE PROBLEM** | The shop is a `Page` without `wide`, so its content column is 46rem inside a 64rem frame — the measure a document is read at, not the width a catalogue is scanned at (`app/shop/page.tsx:72`; `.page` `app/globals.css:363`). Inside it, `.market-list` is a one-column grid of horizontal cards: a 96px thumbnail on the left of a stacked block of four lines (`:1442–1490`). Thirty of those in a column is a list pretending to be a shop. <br><br> The shop has no component for a category, no control for a price band and no link for the next page. Everything the decision documents add — a shelf of sixteen categories, a price row, paging, a link field — has to be built as components and CSS that answer to the frame's tokens (`--measure`, `--space-*`, `--raised`, `--line`, `--text-xs`) rather than to the page's local taste. <br><br> The existing suite (`tests/shop-source.test.tsx`) asserts the old shop: that it searches *headphones* by default, that a card says *2 retailers in these results*, and that the count line reads *nothing the catalogue returned uses*. Each of those is a behaviour the decisions replace, so each test is rewritten to the new behaviour rather than kept beside it. |
| **OPTIONS CONSIDERED (Decision in bold)** | 1. **The shop takes `wide`. A `CategoryShelf` of text tiles sits above the search. Results are a `market-grid` of vertical `MarketCard`s — square image, brand, title, price — two-up on a phone and `auto-fill` at 13rem from 40rem up. A `PriceBands` row of four links and a `ShowMore` link carry the narrowing and paging as URLs. The link field is a small form under the search.** <br> 2. Keep the horizontal row card and give the shop three columns of them. <br> 3. A sidebar of categories beside the grid, as a general retailer does. <br> 4. Client-side filtering and an infinite scroll. |
| **REASONING** | The deciding factor is **what the eye can compare**. A catalogue is scanned, not read: a square image at two-up on a phone puts six products on a screen instead of two, and the price sits directly under the thing it prices. A horizontal card spends half its width on a photograph too small to identify the product and the other half on four lines of text that cannot be compared down a column. Option 2 is the same card at a wider measure; it does not make the grid scannable. <br><br> The second factor is **what the frame already promises**. `wide` exists for screens that are looked at rather than read (IPD 30), and the shelf is exactly that. Reusing it means the shop shares its edge with the front door and the header, so the page reads as a destination rather than a document pinned inside one. Option 3 is rejected because a sidebar narrows the grid to pay for a list that is already at the top of the page, and on a phone it becomes a drawer — a control that hides the navigation it is. <br><br> Option 4 is rejected because the shop's state is a URL in every other respect: the search is a GET form, the paging is a link, and a refresh has to land on the same page. Client filtering would make the price row a button that cannot be shared or gone back to. |
| **TRADEOFFS** | A vertical tile shows less per product than the four-line row did — the retailer count and the spread move to the product page (IPD 35) — so the grid is broader and shallower. <br><br> The category shelf is a grid of words with no images, because the catalogue gives categories no pictures. Sixteen bordered text tiles is a legible shop only if the type and spacing carry it; it cannot be rescued with imagery that does not exist. <br><br> `auto-fill` at 13rem produces four or five columns at 1440 and two on a phone; at awkward widths it can leave one short row, which is normal for a grid and worth looking at rather than engineering around. <br><br> A page reload per page of results costs a catalogue call and loses scroll position. That is the price of the state being a link (IPD 34). |
| **NOTES** | Accessibility is part of the layout, not a pass afterwards: every card is a single link named by the product title, the image's `alt` is the catalogue's own alt text and falls back to the title, the active category and price band carry `aria-current="page"`, the shelf is a `<nav aria-labelledby>` and not a list of buttons, and every target is at least 44px. <br><br> Cards use the catalogue's `cleaned_url` when a listing has one (the cut-out made for grids) and fall back to `url`. <br><br> The existing `.market-list`, `.market-card-link` and `.market-thumb` rules are replaced rather than extended: a layout with two opinions about the same component is how the previous drift started. <br><br> **As built, 2026-11-12 — looked at, and the phone was the thing to fix.** Screenshots of every state at 1440 and 390, light and dark. Two structural findings, both now fixed. *The page had two rhythm systems.* `.page` is a column with a 40px gap, and `.market-search` carried its own 24px vertical margin from when the shop was one flow of blocks — so every boundary between the shelf, the search and the results cost 64px, twice on a phone, which is most of a screen spent between things. The doubled margin is now cancelled for the frame's direct children only, so the front door's copy of the same form (which sits inside a card that supplies no rhythm) is untouched. *And the control row wrapped.* At 390 the field and its button stacked, leaving a button floating at the left under a full-width field; the row wraps on the sum of the flex bases, so the shop's basis drops to 9rem below 40rem and the pair sits on one line. The front door still stacks, and that is correct rather than missed: its form is inside a card, an input's intrinsic minimum is about 200px, and 300px of card minus the button is less than that. <br><br> One measurement worth keeping: at 390 the first product tile now begins about 710px down, inside the first 844px screen, where before the change it was below 1,600px. Both are checkable — the screenshots were taken by forcing `loading="eager"` on every image, because a full-page capture of a lazy grid photographs empty tiles and would have read as a broken catalogue when it was a screenshot artefact. |
