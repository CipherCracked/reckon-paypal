# ITD 26 — One Figure Leads

> **Superseded in part (2026-11-12, IPD 30).** The first figure still leads, and still at display size. What changed is the arrangement around it: the figures are now one ledger at every width — a hairline between rows, the label on the left, the amount on the right edge — rather than a full-width first figure over a two-up grid. The display step also moved into `--text-hero`'s family: `clamp(2.5rem, 1.8rem + 3vw, 3.5rem)` on the first figure, against `clamp(1.75rem, 1.4rem + 1.4vw, 2.5rem)` on the others, with the `data-quoted` trace rule unchanged., and Wears Its Sentence

| ITD 26 — *"Use the first figure's own scale and the seller's rule to make the argument of a commitment card visible before it is read."* |  |
| :---- | :---- |
| **THE PROBLEM** | `FigureGrid` (ITD 25) made the three figures one object, which was the right number of renderers and the wrong typography. Three equal tiles in one two-column grid, every amount at `--text-md`, every note at the same muted size, the arithmetic and the quoted sentence set in the same face at the same weight. On a card about a commitment, the figure that decides — what the whole term costs — sat at the same size as *Charged today* and one grid cell away from the quote that was supposed to justify it. <br><br> The trace, which is the product, read as annotation: a quote in italics under a number, in the same muted colour as *6 payments of $24.99, one a month*. Nothing said the quote and the figure above it were the same object. The surrounding prose, meanwhile, explained the relationship in a sentence — *the cost to leave is quoted from the seller's own words above* — because the layout was not saying it. <br><br> A second, smaller defect came from the same pass: *At least* was printed inline at the display size of the amount, so `$149.94` read as *At least $149.94* in one breath, and the qualifier looked like part of the number. |
| **OPTIONS CONSIDERED (Decision in bold)** | 1. **The first figure gets a band of its own across the card at display size (`clamp(2.25rem, 1.5rem + 3vw, 3.5rem)`); the other two sit two-up beneath it. The figure that carries a sentence is marked `data-quoted` by the grid, and its term and its quote wear the same 3px `--accent` rule, so the quote is visually the figure's own footnote rather than a caption. The quote is set in the body face, the arithmetic stays in the figure face. *At least* becomes a block-level span at `--text-sm`, so the word is a word. Figures lose their individual boxes — the card is the object.** <br> 2. Highlight the deciding figure with a tinted background or a coloured amount. <br> 3. A bar chart comparing the three figures to scale. <br> 4. Drop two of the three figures and show only the term total. |
| **REASONING** | The deciding factor is **what a person scans before reading**. A card about a commitment is entered at its largest number; scale is the one hierarchy signal a reader obeys without being told, and it costs no words. Setting the term total at display size and the others at body size ranks the figures the way the decision ranks them, and it does it in the same language on the entry, the product page, the consent screen and the record — the same component, so it cannot disagree with itself. <br><br> The trace is the second factor, and it is the product's USP, so it cannot be a sentence of explanation. A quote that wears the same rule as the label above it is read as belonging to that figure *before* the reader parses the words; that is the difference between a layout that shows the mechanism and one that describes it. Marking the figure with `data-quoted` rather than styling by position keeps the rule honest: it appears only where a sentence was actually quoted from the seller's words, so a figure that was arithmetic and nothing else is not dressed as evidence. <br><br> `data-quoted` also replaces the alternative of a modifier class per figure id, which would have put the layout in the business of knowing which figure *is* the quoted one — the grid renders what `presentFigures` produced (ITD 19) and nothing more. <br><br> Option 2 is rejected because colour on an amount makes it look like a status — green for good, red for a warning — and this product does not deliver verdicts (IPD 4). Option 3 is rejected because the three figures are not comparable magnitudes: across the term, charged today and cost to leave are different kinds of number, and a bar chart would invite a comparison that is not meaningful. Option 4 removes the one figure a person cannot get anywhere else — the cost to leave — to gain space the card already has. <br><br> The *At least* fix is a correction to the same principle: a qualifier is not a number, so it is not set like one, and setting it as a block above the amount stops the eye reading the two as a single value. |
| **TRADEOFFS** | A display-size number in a grid cell is a wrapping risk on a narrow card, so the first figure spans the full width of the card at every width and the two-up grid only holds the two smaller amounts. <br><br> The card is now dense with one very large element; on the record, where the same grid renders compact, the hierarchy is deliberately absent — a row in a list is not a page, so it keeps the label, the amount and the arithmetic and no quotation. <br><br> The rule carries meaning only because it appears twice, on the term and on the quote; a future card that quotes a sentence without a quoted figure would look inconsistent, so anything that adds a quote has to go through `presentFigures`. <br><br> `— the seller's label` sits inside the quote's block, and on a narrow card a long label wraps under the rule, which reads slightly as if the label were also quoted. It is set in the muted colour and not italic to keep it distinct; at 30rem the wrap is acceptable rather than tidy. |
| **NOTES** | The quote is not clamped. A quoted sentence shortened to fit is no longer the span that was checked, and the product's claim is that the words shown are the ones the figure came from. <br><br> `--accent` is the same token the focus ring and the primary button use; the rule is 3px so it survives at the display size above it without drawing the eye away from the number. |
